# The Human + AI Workshop

### A 75-year-old guy, a collection of old computers, an increasingly ridiculous home lab, and an AI assistant trying to build useful things.

This is the notebook of an ongoing experiment.

There is no venture capital. No team of twenty engineers. No product roadmap.

There is me.

There are a few computers that probably should have been retired years ago. There is a Proxmox server that has acquired more jobs than I originally intended to give it. There are ESP32 boards, sensors, printers, hard drives, containers, Bluetooth devices, assorted cables, and enough experimental software to make the whole thing increasingly difficult to explain to normal people.

And there is an AI assistant.

I started using ChatGPT because I wanted help figuring things out.

Somewhere along the way, "help figuring things out" turned into actually building things.

We write code. We break code. We figure out why it broke. We try something else. Sometimes the solution works immediately.

More often, it doesn't.

That's actually the interesting part.

This journal is an attempt to keep track of what happens.

Not just the finished applications, but the thinking that led to them:

- What was I trying to accomplish?
- What did I think would work?
- What actually happened?
- What did the AI suggest?
- What did I accept, reject, or change?
- What did we learn?

The AI writes a lot of the code, but it doesn't own the project. I decide what I'm trying to accomplish, make the design decisions, test things in the real world, and occasionally tell the AI that its brilliant solution has once again managed to make something considerably worse.

That seems to be a reasonably productive arrangement.

So this is the workshop notebook.

Some days will produce a working application.

Some days will produce a useful script.

Some days will produce three hours of troubleshooting and one line of configuration that fixes everything.

And occasionally, we may discover that the entire idea was wrong.

Those days belong here too.

## Why Keep a Journal?

Because otherwise the workshop has no memory.

A problem gets solved and six months later I've forgotten why we did it that way.

A little program turns into a useful application.

A crazy idea becomes a real project.

Something that seemed impossible turns out to be a couple of hours of experimentation.

And sometimes I discover that the thing I was trying to accomplish was actually much simpler than the solution I had imagined.

The journal is the record of that process.

Not a polished story of everything going according to plan.

More like the actual story.

**This is what I tried.**

**This is what happened.**

**This is what we built.**
